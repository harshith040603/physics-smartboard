The black body · Everyday examples
Why a small opening looks black
Light that goes in bounces around inside and is mostly absorbed. Very little comes back out to your eye.
The pupil of your eye
Light entering the pupil is absorbed inside the eye. Almost none comes back, so it looks black.
A window, seen from far away
In daylight, light entering the window is absorbed inside the room. Little comes back, so it looks dark.

The black body · A common mistake
“Black body” does not mean black colour
“Black” means it absorbs all radiation. When hot enough, a black body gives out visible light and glows (temperature rising, left to right).
Cool · looks black
Hot · glows red
Hotter · glows yellow
Very hot · glows white

1
a + r + t = 1
Radiation falling on a surface is split into absorbed, reflected and transmitted parts.
2
Absorptive power, a
a = energy absorbed ÷ energy incident. No unit, 0 ≤ a ≤ 1. a = 0.8 means 80 % is absorbed.
3
Emissive power, E
Energy radiated per unit area per unit time. Unit W/m². Depends strongly on temperature.
4
Emissivity, e
e = E ÷ E of a black body at the same T. No unit, 0 ≤ e ≤ 1.

Note it down
Part 2b — write this in your notebook
1
Perfect black body
Absorbs all radiation falling on it: a = 1, r = 0, t = 0. Best possible emitter: e = 1.
2
Practical black body
A small hole in a hollow cavity. The hole is the black body, not the whole box.
3
Why openings look dark
Light going in is reflected again and again and absorbed. Very little comes back out.
4
Black ≠ black colour
“Black” means perfect absorber. A very hot black body can glow red, yellow or white.
5
JEE trap
For a black body e = 1, not E = 1. E has unit W/m² and depends on T.

Final revision
Parts 1 & 2 in ten lines
1
Radiation is energy transfer by electromagnetic waves. It needs no material medium.
2
Every body above 0 K emits radiation continuously.
3
At thermal equilibrium, emission and absorption carry on but are equal, so net heat transfer is zero.
4
When radiation falls on a surface: a + r + t = 1.
5
Absorptive power a is a fraction between 0 and 1, with no unit.
6
Emissive power E is energy radiated per unit area per unit time. Unit: W/m².
7
Emissivity e compares a surface with a perfect emitter at the same T; 0 ≤ e ≤ 1.
8
A perfectly black body has a = 1 and e = 1.
9
A small hole in a cavity is the practical model of a black body.
10
“Black body” is about absorption, not visible black colour.
