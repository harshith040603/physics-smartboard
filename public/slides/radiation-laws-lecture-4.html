Kirchhoff · The argument
Start from thermal equilibrium
sealed, insulated enclosure
A at T
B at T
Left for a long time: everything reaches the same T
If a body absorbed more than it emitted, its temperature would rise.
If it emitted more than it absorbed, its temperature would fall.
At equilibrium neither happens, so the rates balance:
emitted per second = absorbed per second

Kirchhoff · The law
Kirchhoff's law: e = a
The statement
E / a = E of a black body = σT⁴
For any body at a given temperature.
Why e = a
e = E / E(black body)
Kirchhoff: E = a · E(black body)
⇒
e = a
At a given temperature, a good absorber is a good emitter, and a poor absorber is a poor emitter.
Wavelength by wavelength too: a surface that absorbs a wavelength well emits that same wavelength well when hot.

Kirchhoff · Seeing it
A black spot on a white plate
Cold, in daylight
The black spot looks darker: it absorbs more of the light.
Heated, in a dark room
The same spot glows brighter: it emits more.
Red glass lets red through and absorbs the other colours strongly. When heated, it glows greenish: it emits most strongly the colours it absorbed.

Stefan · Spheres
The sphere shortcut
For a sphere
A = 4πr²
P ∝ r²T⁴
For two states of the same kind of sphere (same e):
P₂ / P₁ = (r₂ / r₁)² (T₂ / T₁)⁴
Ratio rule: before you use σ or convert units, check whether it is a ratio question. Most quantities cancel.

Stefan · Net radiation
The surroundings radiate too
surroundings at T₀
body at T
emitted: eσAT⁴
absorbed: eσAT₀⁴
Pₙₑₜ = eσA(T⁴ − T₀⁴)
Using Kirchhoff: a = e, so the body absorbs eσAT₀⁴.
T > T₀: net heat flows out of the body.
T = T₀: Pₙₑₜ = 0, but both still happen.
T ≫ T₀: the T₀⁴ term can be neglected.

Wien + Stefan
Using both laws together
If T doubles
λₘ → λₘ / 2 (Wien)
area under curve → 16 × area (Stefan)
Spheres or discs, equal e
P ∝ r²T⁴ and T ∝ 1/λₘ
⇒
P ∝ r² / λₘ⁴
Method: Wien turns the peak wavelength into a temperature. Stefan turns temperature and area into power.

28
Watch out
Five common mistakes in Wien questions
1
Writing λₘ ∝ T. It is inverse: λₘ ∝ 1/T.
2
Forgetting that a hotter curve must peak at a shorter wavelength.
3
Using only the radius, or only the wavelength, in a combined power problem.
4
Dropping the fourth power: with T ∝ 1/λₘ, power ∝ 1/λₘ⁴.
5
Thinking the peak is the only wavelength emitted. A black body emits a broad spectrum.
